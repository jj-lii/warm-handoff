# 0009 Problem: long-stay residents denied short-term skilled care under Medicare Advantage
Status: accepted
Date: 2026-10-04
Context: ADR 0008 kept MA denials/appeals (#1) and admission-rule tuning (#2). The author wanted a long-tail problem: outside the core 20% ExaCare is already pursuing, valuable, not low-hanging, weekend-sized. A general payer compliance check sits on ExaCare's announced appeals/NOMNC path. The full OIG report (OEI-09-24-00331) shows long-stay residents denied at 39.5% vs 11.5%, with insurers citing residency and ability to participate in therapy, which conflicts with Medicare Benefit Policy Manual Ch. 8 (daily skilled nursing qualifies; maintenance is covered per Jimmo) and CMS-4201-F (same criteria for all). Help articles show ExaCare's flow starts from a hospital referral; in-house residents need a Quick Upload "referral".
Decision: Problem = long-stay nursing home residents on MA who need a short-term skilled stay are denied about 4x as often, on reasoning that conflicts with Medicare's coverage rules, in a workflow built for hospital referrals. Product direction: a Chapter 8 check for this flow (build the request to pre-empt the known reasoning; check denial reasons against Ch. 8 and CMS-4201-F; draft the response), plus a real-document comparison of plans' published SNF policies against Ch. 8.
Alternatives: General payer compliance check (rejected as the lead: on ExaCare's announced path, fairly obvious); rule elicitation/maintenance (service-shaped; ADR 0008 discussion); appeals drafting (already announced by ExaCare); hospital alliance (hard to prototype in a weekend).
Consequences: Narrow segment (about 3% of SNF requests in OIG's sample), so the value story is per facility and per patient, not market size. Rests on V17 (no in-house prior-auth start point) and on OIG reporting insurers' explanations rather than denial-level reasons. Synthetic patient cases only (ADR 0004); real public documents (Ch. 8, plan policies) are allowed as they hold no patient data.
